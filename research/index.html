        Correcting for the number of ideas tried

        If N worthless variants are tried, the best still shows a positive Sharpe ratio. Its expected size (Bailey & López de Prado, 2014) is

        E[max SR] ≈ √V · [ (1−γ)·Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γ·Φ⁻¹(1−1/(N·e)) ]

        about 2.5 standard deviations at 100 trials and 3.5 at 3,000. The deflated Sharpe ratio is the probability that a model's Sharpe ratio beats that bar, given the sample length, skew and kurtosis:

        DSR = Φ( (SR − SR₀)·√(T−1) / √(1 − γ₃·SR + (γ₄−1)/4·SR²) )

        Because the true count of ideas tried is uncertain, each model reports it at 100 and at 3,000 trials. The probability of backtest overfitting (Bailey et al., 2017) asks how often the in-sample winner ranks below the median out of sample.

          Assumptions

          All performance on this page is hypothetical. Results to 23 October 2026 are backtests on historical one-minute data with simulated fills; they are not realised trading profits. Fills on fast days can be worse than one tick. Results since 23 October 2026 are a forward record kept under frozen rules, still simulated on each day's data rather than broker fills. Nothing here is investment advice.
